State AI laws, 2026

A map of the 115 AI measures that 30 states passed or sent to governors in 2026, plus documented vetoed and failed bills, and a chart that lines those laws up against a four-step transparency framework for automated decision systems (ADS) in public benefits programs.

Working draft. The laws come from the Transparency Coalition's 2026 State AI Legislation Report. The framework comes from the policy paper in this project. Almost none of the 2026 laws cover government benefits systems, so most matches are analogues, not equivalents. See About.

About this site

Sources

  • Laws: Transparency Coalition, 2026 State AI Legislation Report (published Oct. 27, 2026; legislation data from LegiScan). It counts bills signed or passed and awaiting signature as of Oct. 15, 2026, and excludes vetoed bills. Updated Oct. 29, 2026: Pennsylvania SB 806 (signed Oct. 22, Act 99) and New York S 9051 (signed Oct. 28) are now shown as enacted. Five other New York bills still await Gov. Hochul, who has until Dec. 31.
  • Vetoed, failed, pending and in-session bills: LegiScan bulk legislative datasets (CSV Basic) for every 2026 state session, exported Aug. to Oct. 2026. Bills are screened for AI terms in the title and the start of the description, then sorted by LegiScan's official status. Each row links to its LegiScan page. A build script in tools/build_outcomes.py regenerates data/outcomes.js from the downloads.
  • Framework: the recommended policy framework in the fellowship paper: (1) public inventory of ADS, (2) individual notice and explanation, (3) impact assessment, testing and monitoring, (4) vendor and developer obligations, plus design choices on scope, covered decisions and enforcement. It borrows from federal OMB M-25-21 and New York's LOADinG Act.

How laws were matched to the framework

The author read each law's summary in the report and tagged the framework step it most resembles. Close analogue means the law imposes a similar duty (for example, Indiana's HB 1271 requires insurers to disclose AI use in a denial and offer an appeal, which resembles Step 2). Loose analogue means a related idea in a different setting (for example, chatbot disclosure laws resemble notice). Summaries are short, so some laws are tagged conservatively. The tags are judgments, not findings in the report. Please check a bill's text before relying on a tag.

What the chart shows about the gap

The 2026 wave is dominated by chatbot safety, kids' social media, deepfakes and health insurance. None of the enacted or pending entries creates a public inventory of government benefits ADS, and none gives benefits applicants a notice of automated decision-making with an explanation right. The closest analogues are in health insurance (human review and AI-use disclosure), frontier-model audits (Illinois SB 315, California SB 813 and AB 1405) and chatbot reporting (Rhode Island). Colorado's SB 189 moved the other way by removing annual impact assessments.

Limits

  • The map shows 2026 only. Earlier laws are not on it.
  • Not a complete count. The Transparency Coalition's 115 is its own tally. NCSL reportedly counts about 280 AI laws enacted in 2026 because it casts a wider net (for example deepfake and government-use measures), but I could not open NCSL's database to confirm its scope. Treat this site as a view of the Coalition's set plus the extras listed here.
  • Keyword screen. Failed, vetoed and in-session bills are found by keyword (such as "artificial intelligence", "chatbot", "deepfake", "algorithm"). That catches most AI bills but also some unrelated ones, and misses bills whose titles never mention AI. Treat counts as approximate.
  • Snapshot dates. LegiScan files were exported between summer and Oct. 25, 2026, so very recent signings can lag. I corrected New York S 9051 (signed Oct. 28) and Pennsylvania SB 806 (signed Oct. 22) by hand.
  • Died means the session ended. A bill still introduced or passed one chamber when its legislature adjourned counts as failed. Massachusetts, Michigan, Ohio, New Jersey, North Carolina, Pennsylvania and D.C. are still in session, so their bills show as "still in session".
  • Extra enacted laws. The 26 enacted bills beyond the Coalition's list (many in Tennessee and Louisiana) were found by the keyword screen and checked by title. I tagged them to the framework by hand from LegiScan's short descriptions, so those tags are the least certain. New York S 8831 is tagged from a one-line description and should be checked against the bill text.
